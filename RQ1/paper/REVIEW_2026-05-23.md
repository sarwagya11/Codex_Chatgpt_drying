## Phase 1 — Independent reviewer reports

### R0. Editor-in-Chief

**Recommendation:** Major Revision (conditional on §5.3 onward and §6 being supplied)
**Confidence:** 4/5
**Summary.** The manuscript simulates ten solar-assisted heat-pump-dryer topologies for apple at four Nepali sites and ranks them on SEC at a fixed 45 °C set point. The simulation infrastructure is detailed (§3), the topology comparison in §5.2 is the strongest stretch of writing in the draft and supports a clear design rule, and the choice of fixing recirculation fraction at r=0 to isolate topology is methodologically clean. Journal fit is acceptable: Renewable Energy publishes SAHPD simulation studies of this scope, and the Kuan et al. 2019 style anchor is appropriate. The current weaknesses are (i) the framing in §1 is overlong and the novelty claim is geographic (first Nepal evaluation) layered onto a topology comparison whose own novelty needs sharper articulation, (ii) the system-level model has no experimental validation reported in the draft, only internal energy/mass-balance closure, (iii) §5 is incomplete and the headline ranking has not yet been argued through to a publishable contribution.

**Top three editorial concerns.**
1. Sharpen the contribution. Three contributions are listed in §1¶4; the topology-ranking contribution is real (Tables 4a, 4b are good), but the framing buries it. Identify which contribution carries the paper and lead with that.
2. Validation against an external benchmark is missing. For a Q1 journal, the cycle and the collector should be validated against either a published SAHPD experiment or against the authors' own bench data; otherwise the SEC numbers are unanchored.
3. §1¶4 promises a five-parameter Arrhenius-humidity-velocity-thickness kinetic law with profile-likelihood confidence intervals on the activation energy, but §1¶2 also frames "no propagation of kinetic uncertainty into SEC" as a literature gap; the paper as drafted reports SEC as a point estimate, so the gap that motivates the contribution is not closed by the contribution.

### R1. Methodology reviewer (vapor-compression and simulation)

**Recommendation:** Major Revision
**Confidence:** 5/5
**Summary.** §3 is thorough and the equation set is internally consistent at the algebra level: cycle states 1 to 4, effectiveness/pinch closures for both coils, HWB collector with thermal inertia, sensible HRX with dewpoint check on the hot side, iterative makeup-air for D2/E2/E3, and a per-tray air-capacity gate. Two global balances (Q_cond = Q_evap + W_comp; cumulative water mass) close to numerical precision, which is good housekeeping. The weaknesses are concentrated around parameter choices, identifiability of the kinetic fit, and missing external validation.

**Strengths.**

- S1. Subsystem decomposition is clean and the math is reproducible from the manuscript. The four cycle states, Eqs. (1)-(10), are written out at a level that another modeller could replicate without code access.
- S2. The per-tray air-capacity gate (Eqs. 24-27) is a genuine modelling subtlety that is rarely written out explicitly in SAHPD papers and is correctly handled here, including the bisection on Δω_max.
- S3. The variable-T_cond control for B2 and E3 (Eqs. 39-41) is non-trivial and the algebra is correctly tied back to the effectiveness/pinch system. The solar-priority cutoff at α_e=1 is the right boundary.
- S4. The iterative evaporator sizing for D2, E2, E3 (Eqs. 42-47) is the correct treatment for the cooled-exhaust + ambient supplement architecture. Termination tolerance (1 W) and iteration cap (20) are sensible.

**Weaknesses.**

- W1. **No external experimental validation reported.** *Problem:* The draft reports only internal first-law and water-mass closure; both are tautological for an explicit Euler integrator with a consistent enthalpy formulation. *Why it matters:* This kind of paper is normally read against either bench data from the authors' own rig or a published benchmark (e.g., Mortezapour et al., Yahya & Fudholi). Without an external anchor, the SEC numbers in Tables 4a/4b can only be compared internally. *Suggestion:* Either add a §3.13 validation subsection benchmarking the cycle/collector against a published configuration whose parameters are recoverable, or explicitly acknowledge this as a stated limitation and justify the internal-consistency stance. *Severity:* Critical.

- W2. **Heat exchanger effectiveness values are on the optimistic side.** *Problem:* ε_cond = ε_evap = 0.85 with 10 K pinch (Table 2) is a strong combination for air-coupled refrigerant coils; typical published values for compact fin-tube SAHPD coils sit in 0.60-0.80. *Why it matters:* The whole COP/Q_cond ladder is sensitive to this choice and the topology ranking may depend on it. *Suggestion:* Either justify ε = 0.85 with a coil-sizing argument (UA / face area / fin geometry) or run a sensitivity sweep over ε ∈ {0.70, 0.80, 0.85} and report whether the E2 > E3 > E1 ranking survives. *Severity:* Major.

- W3. **Kinetic-fit identifiability is not demonstrated.** *Problem:* §3.4.3 says profile-likelihood CIs on Ea will be reported in §5, but with five parameters (K_ref, Ea, α_RH, γ_v, δ_d) fit jointly against 13 curves spanning narrow T (40-50 °C) and v (0.6-1.1 m/s) ranges, parameter correlations are likely large and any single CI may be misleading. *Why it matters:* The runtime simulator extrapolates outside the calibration band (e.g., evaporator side air is well below 40 °C); identifiability shows whether the K(T, RH, v, d) surface is constrained where the simulation actually evaluates it. *Suggestion:* Report the full 5x5 parameter correlation matrix and joint confidence regions for the (Ea, α_RH) and (γ_v, δ_d) pairs; flag any region where the kinetic engine evaluates K outside the calibration band. *Severity:* Major.

- W4. **Cross-reference errors in §3.4.3.** *Problem:* §3.4.3 line 1 reads "The five parameters of Eq. (20)..." but the rate law is Eq. (22); the same paragraph then says "compatibility with the per-step first-order moisture update of Eq. (21)" but Eq. (21) is the absorber-plate inertia equation, not the moisture update (which is Eq. 23). *Why it matters:* The reader has to backtrack and verify which equation is meant; it also undermines confidence that other algebra has been independently checked. *Suggestion:* Replace both references; verify all cross-references in §3.4 line by line against the renumbered equation set. *Severity:* Major.

- W5. **§3.10 fan-power text contradicts Table 2.** *Problem:* §3.10 ends "the fan-efficiency η_fan is held constant across configurations to isolate the topology effect from any blower-class dependence; this is the only fan parameter that does not appear in Table 2 as a per-component number." But Table 2 lists η_fan = 0.60 under "Fan & ducts". *Why it matters:* Trivial but visible. *Suggestion:* Rewrite as "this is the only fan parameter that is not a per-component ΔP value" or remove the parenthetical altogether. *Severity:* Minor.

- W6. **First-law closure is tautological, not informative.** *Problem:* §3.6 reports that Q_cond = Q_evap + W_comp closes "to numerical precision" as a consistency test. With explicit Euler at dt=60 s and a single-pass refrigerant enthalpy formulation, this closure is a tautology of the discretisation, not evidence of correctness. *Why it matters:* Presenting it as validation overstates the strength of the check. *Suggestion:* Reframe as a numerical-consistency safeguard rather than a model validation; reserve the word "validation" for external benchmarks. *Severity:* Minor.

**Questions to authors.**
1. Was a sensitivity analysis performed on ε_cond, ε_evap, ΔT_sh, ΔT_sc? If yes, is the E2 > E3 > E1 > D > B > A > 0 ordering robust?
2. Where does the simulated K(T, RH, v, d) evaluate outside the calibration band of Royen et al.? (E.g., at the evaporator pre-cooling stage the local T can drop well below 40 °C.)
3. What is the rationale for picking a 1-ton split-AC frame (Q_cond,max = 4.0 kW) as the upper duty bound, and how often do timesteps cross it across the 256 cases?

### R2. Domain reviewer (SAHPD literature and apple kinetics)

**Recommendation:** Major Revision
**Confidence:** 4/5
**Summary.** Literature coverage is adequate in coverage of the SAHPD canon (Aktaş, Mortezapour, Yahya & Fudholi, Rulazi, Kuan, Qiu, Ismaeel & Yumrutaş) and the apple kinetic reference (Royen et al.) is well chosen. The gap statement is structurally clean (three gaps) but the third gap, the matched-set-point topology comparison, is the load-bearing one and could be argued more sharply. The novelty bookkeeping is uneven: the Solar+HP+HRX combination is correctly identified as not novel, but the topology comparison itself, which is the actual technical contribution, is under-defended against the prior Vega & Cuevas, Mortezapour, and Kuan studies that already vary collector placement.

**Strengths.**

- S1. The kinetics gap (single-source, no propagation of uncertainty) is a legitimate and rarely-articulated gap, and the choice to refit from 1573 cleaned moisture-ratio points across 13 curves is a credible upgrade over single-temperature Arrhenius fits.
- S2. The selection of four Nepali sites spanning 1.7 km elevation across three climate bands is well motivated for a country-specific paper.
- S3. §5.2's B1 vs C1 comparison at fixed Q_solar is exactly the controlled experiment the topology question needs, and the design rule that emerges ("place the collector so it sees the coldest available chamber-supply air and substitutes directly for condenser duty") is original framing, transferable beyond Nepal.

**Weaknesses.**

- W1. **The novelty claim is fundamentally geographic.** *Problem:* §1¶3 leads with "no peer-reviewed evaluation of HPD or SAHPD has been reported for Nepal". This is a defensible gap but it is a country gap, not a technical gap. The technical contribution (matched-set-point ten-topology ranking) is then framed as a sub-claim. *Why it matters:* Renewable Energy will receive this as "yet another SAHPD simulation" unless the topology contribution is foregrounded. The Vega & Cuevas (2020) citation that appears once in §5.2 already varies collector placement for an air-source SAHP; the manuscript needs to delineate clearly what this study adds beyond Vega & Cuevas. *Suggestion:* Restructure §1¶4 to lead with the topology contribution and treat the Nepal climate envelope as the test bed in which the contribution is validated, not as the contribution itself. *Severity:* Critical.

- W2. **The "no kinetic uncertainty propagation" gap is stated but not closed.** *Problem:* §1¶2 closes by criticising prior SAHPD studies for using single-source kinetic fits "without propagating the resulting parameter uncertainty into the simulated SEC". §5 as drafted reports SEC as a point estimate (Tables 4a, 4b are deterministic). *Why it matters:* The headline gap announces an analytical advance that the manuscript does not deliver. Reviewers will catch this. *Suggestion:* Either (a) add Monte Carlo propagation of the kinetic CIs into SEC and report 95% intervals on the headline numbers in Tables 3, 4a, 4b, or (b) demote this from a gap to a methodological observation and rewrite §1¶2 accordingly. Option (a) is publishable as a contribution; option (b) is a defensible retreat. *Severity:* Critical.

- W3. **Internal numerical inconsistency between §1 and §4.2.** *Problem:* §1¶4 lists "four Nepali sites at 72 to 1820 m elevation"; §4.2 reports the same four sites at 80, 1302, 1482, 1783 m. The endpoints disagree (72 vs 80; 1820 vs 1783). The auto-memory record from the simulator gives 72, 1350, 1550, 1820 m. *Why it matters:* A reviewer who checks coordinates against site gazetteers will find the discrepancy and lose trust. *Suggestion:* Reconcile against the code's `config_solar_hp.py` values (the simulator is the source of truth) and propagate the same numbers through §1, §4.2, and any figure captions. *Severity:* Major.

- W4. **Season list disagreement between §1 and §4.2.** *Problem:* §1¶4 says "Annual and seasonal (monsoon, winter, autumn, spring) subsets are retained". §4.2 lists "autumn, winter, spring, summer" with the summer window described as "pre-monsoon irradiance peak". There is no "monsoon" season in the simulation matrix as §4.2 defines it. *Why it matters:* Same trust issue as W3; also makes the introduction promise something the methods section does not deliver. *Suggestion:* Align both to the labels actually run; if monsoon (July-September) is excluded because apples are not dried in monsoon, state that explicitly in §4.2. *Severity:* Major.

- W5. **Aktaş 2015 diffusivity comparison is over-precise without the underlying conditions.** *Problem:* §1¶2 reports "effective diffusivity of 2.36 × 10⁻⁸ m²/s, more than twice that of a solar-dryer comparator". Without the slice geometry, drying temperature, and the diffusion model assumed, this single number does not support the "HPD accelerates apple drying" claim cleanly. *Why it matters:* Reviewers familiar with the literature will read this as headline cherry-picking. *Suggestion:* Either pair the diffusivity with the temperature and thickness at which it was measured, or replace with a less load-bearing citation and move the diffusivity number to a results comparison in §5. *Severity:* Major.

- W6. **The kinetic dataset is itself single-source.** *Problem:* §1¶2 frames the "single experimental source per fit" practice as a gap; the manuscript then fits its own five-parameter law against the single Royen et al. dataset. *Why it matters:* The criticism applies to this paper too unless the wording is amended. *Suggestion:* Reframe the gap as "no uncertainty propagation into SEC" (process gap) rather than "single experimental source" (provenance gap), so the contribution and the gap are aligned. *Severity:* Major.

**Questions to authors.**
1. What does this study contribute beyond Vega & Cuevas 2020 (cited in §5.2), which already varies collector placement on an air-source SAHP? The reader needs one sentence in §1 to differentiate.
2. Does the K(T, RH, v, d) law extrapolate cleanly to the chamber states that the simulator actually evaluates, or are there cases where the rate constant is being read off outside the calibration band?
3. Why was Royen et al. selected over the other apple thin-layer datasets cited in §1¶2 (Sharabiani et al. 2021)? Is the choice driven by raw-data availability, by the breadth of the (T, v, d) grid, or both?

### R4. Devil's Advocate

**Strongest counter-argument (220 words).**

The headline result, that E2 (HRX + pre-condenser solar) beats every other configuration on annual SEC at every site, may be a parameter-choice artefact rather than a topological truth. Four parameter choices co-determine the ranking: ε_cond = ε_evap = 0.85 (high), ΔT_pinch = 10 K (typical), η_is = 0.75 (typical), and a fixed T_set = 45 °C with a fixed compressor nameplate that none of the cases approach saturating. Each one is defensible in isolation but their combination biases the simulator toward solutions in which the HP cycle has plenty of headroom and the marginal cost of adding a recovery component is small. Under more realistic coil effectiveness (ε ~ 0.70) and a tighter compressor envelope, the cooled-exhaust route in D2/E2/E3 may force the makeup-air fan power up to the point where the iterative-sizing penalty erodes the HRX gain. The "88% of the sum" sub-additivity claim in §5.1 is then re-readable as "weakly antagonistic when ε is large" rather than as a robust general result. The topology ranking is plausible, even likely correct, but the manuscript has not yet shown it survives a perturbation of these four parameters. Until that sensitivity analysis appears, the ranking is conditional on a particular and optimistic operating envelope.

**Issue list.**

- **DA1 (Critical).** Topology ranking robustness to ε_cond, ε_evap, ΔT_pinch is not demonstrated. The whole §5.1 family ranking and the §5.2 placement rule rest on a single point in the (ε, ΔT_pinch) plane.
- **DA2 (Critical).** The "no kinetic-uncertainty propagation" gap is announced (§1¶2) but not closed by the manuscript (§5 reports point-estimate SEC). This is a self-undermining contradiction the reader will find within thirty seconds of skimming the paper.
- **DA3 (Major).** The "drying time is identical (14.4 h) across all configurations" claim in §5.1 means the topology comparison is entirely a comparison of W_electric at fixed schedule. The paper should own this explicitly: the headline metric is *energy cost per kg water at fixed residence time*, not *drying performance*. As written, this is implicit and may mislead readers expecting throughput differences.
- **DA4 (Major).** The geographic novelty ("first Nepal SAHPD study") risks being read as the only novelty once the technical contributions are scrutinised. The Solar+HP+HRX architecture is not new (Qiu 2017, Ismaeel & Yumrutaş cited), and the placement comparison is anticipated by Vega & Cuevas 2020. The paper needs a one-sentence delta against Vega & Cuevas.
- **DA5 (Major).** The contribution claim "transferable across Nepal's elevation gradient" is not supported with cross-validation. Four sites is a sparse sample of a country with high microclimatic heterogeneity (Karnali far-west, Annapurna mid-hill, Khumbu high-altitude all unsampled). Either retreat to "across the four climate bands sampled" or add a leave-one-site-out cross-validation of the ranking.
- **DA6 (Major).** §3 declares ε_cond and ε_evap as separate parameters but Table 2 sets them both at 0.85. If they are physically coupled (same coil class), one is enough; if independent, the rationale for both at 0.85 deserves a sentence.
- **DA7 (Minor).** The "fan-power model" (§3.10) implicitly assumes blower-class invariance across the 0.5-1.5 kW shaft-power range that the configurations actually demand. This is fine as a simplification but should be flagged.

**Ignored alternative explanations.** The placement-rule story in §5.2 ("solar should see the coldest air") is consistent with the data but is not the only available reading. The alternative reading is that B2/E3 underperform because the post-condenser collector inlet is fixed at T_set = 45 °C and the HWB heat-loss term (U_L · (T_pl − T_amb)) penalises any plate above ambient; the rule is then "solar should see the largest (T_amb − T_pl) gap", which is a statement about collector physics rather than about heat-pump topology. Mathematically the two readings are equivalent at r=0 but they prescribe different design moves when r > 0 or when ambient T rises in summer. Worth flagging.

**Missing stakeholder perspectives.** The end user is the cooperative operator (energy cost), but two other stakeholders are not represented: the equipment installer (HRX retrofit cost, blower sizing) and the apple producer (drying-air quality affecting product colour, aroma; not modelled here because the kinetic engine only tracks moisture).

**"So what?" test.** If a Nepali cooperative read §1, §5.2, and a §6 they have not yet written, would they know whether to buy the E2 system or the cheaper A system? At the current draft state, no, because the SEC saving is not translated into payback. The paper as drafted answers the engineering "which topology is most efficient?" question; it does not yet answer the practitioner "is it worth building?" question.

It is currently held back by three critical concerns (C1, C2, C3), six major concerns (C4 to C9), and one minor (C10). ## Notes on writing quality

A non-blocking observation, separable from the substantive findings. Three patterns recur and depress readability:

- **Very long sentences.** §1¶1 sentence 1 is approximately 80 words. §1¶2 sentence 1 is 60 words. §3.4.2 has a single sentence spanning Eqs. 24-27 that runs for the full paragraph. Splitting at the natural semicolon boundary into two shorter sentences raises comprehension without loss of register.
- **Compound modifier stacks.** "Heat-pump-plus-heat-recovery-exchanger-based drying configurations" (§1¶4), "Arrhenius-humidity-velocity-thickness rate law" (§3.4), "pool-adjacent-violators isotonic cleaning" (§3.4.3) are examples. The Renewable Energy register tolerates one stacked modifier per noun phrase but several per sentence is unusual.
- **Passive voice as default.** The style anchor pins passive voice, which is fine for methods sections; in §1 and §5 narrative passages, the same default produces sentences whose subject is hard to recover (e.g., "Against this backdrop, a numerical investigation has been undertaken in the present work to identify..." in §1¶4). One pass replacing "has been undertaken" with "this work identifies" where it does not violate the style anchor will tighten the prose.

These are not blocking issues for the technical content. They will be picked up by Renewable Energy's language review at production but are easier and cheaper to fix during this revision.
